What's new in Strandivo

Release notes for every version of Strandivo, newest first.

0.4.0

Mark domains, linkers and CDRs in a coding sequence as protein regions, see them on the map, and copy or save them as a figure. The Reads tab copes with hundreds of reads and keeps the reference in view, and primer design can move ends onto better stretches. Ctrl+S works again on Windows, and if Strandivo ever quits unexpectedly it offers to report the crash.

  • Protein regions: mark domains, helices, linkers, CDRs and frameworks within a CDS, nested if need be, from more kinds or categories of your own. Select bases within a CDS and choose Add as region… in the inspector or the right-click menu, and the residue positions are filled in; Get from UniProt… now offers domains, repeats and other regions too. New regions get colours of their own near their CDS's, and colours you choose are kept. Regions show in a protein diagram, in the outline, as coloured bars with their names in the Sequence view and around the selected primer, and on the map as stripes in their colours along the edge of their CDS; the Regions button chooses all of them, the top level or none, and hovering one highlights its CDS. Expand… draws the protein as a figure, with crowded names on elbow leaders and nested regions bracketed under their parts, to copy into a slide or save as a PNG or SVG. A region can be marked to vary between clones of a library, with its known versions written as DNA bases or as amino acids, and Export can include regions as ordinary annotations in a GenBank copy for other software.
  • Library versions: a clone can be matched to the closest of a region's versions, or only to one it is exactly, which suits a large designed library; anything else is flagged. Versions equally close to a clone are all named instead of the first one listed. Finding a clone among many thousands of versions is much faster, the region's settings warn when a long list would still be slow, and the Reads tab shows how far it has got instead of stopping while it matches. A long list shows in the inspector by its count, with Edit versions… for the whole list.
  • Select codons from translation: click an amino acid in the Sequence view or around a primer to select its DNA bases, or drag to select whole codons. Hold ⌘ on Mac or Ctrl elsewhere to pick separate amino acids, and Shift to select through the intervening residues. Double-click an amino acid or click the feature name to select the whole feature. Copying or deleting separate codons leaves the bases between them alone.
  • Partial coding sequences: a CDS that starts mid-codon (GenBank's codon_start, as in many database records) is now translated in its own frame, in the Sequence view, Copy protein, and the amino-acid changes reported for reads. Saving keeps qualifiers that hold numbers, such as codon_start, and web addresses in notes, which were being dropped.
  • GC content along the sequence: a thin strip under the bases shows where GC leaves 40–60%, teal where it is AT-rich and orange where it is GC-rich, so you can see where a primer will sit well before you draw it. It is on in the Primers tab's sequence panel and turned on with the GC button in the Sequence tab, where the bar at the top also shows GC across the whole sequence.
  • Notes on features: the inspector has a Note field for the selected feature, saved in the file as its GenBank note, and lists the feature's other details from the file, such as gene and database links. Notes with quotes, or long enough to wrap, now open and save whole: a quote used to cut the rest of a line off, and words at a line break were run together. Long notes saved by earlier versions, which split them mid-word, open whole too.
  • Pasting a sequence: text copied from a database or another program can be pasted whole — a FASTA header, a GenBank record, numbered lines — and only its bases go in. Letters that aren't bases used to be pasted as DNA; now Strandivo says the clipboard isn't a sequence instead. U is read as T.
  • Delete with a feature or primer selected now deletes only the feature or primer, as the inspector's Delete button does, and leaves its bases selected: press Delete again to delete them too. Before, one press deleted the bases, and the feature went with them. A cut site or an ORF comes from the sequence, so the first press only makes it a plain selection of bases.
  • Messages about edits have an Undo button. Deleting a feature or primer says so with Undo beside it, and deleting or replacing bases now says when a feature or primer that lay wholly inside them was deleted too, which was easy to miss. A new message about an edit replaces the last one instead of piling up, at most three messages show at once, and one with a button, or an error, stays while the pointer is over it and can be closed.
  • My enzymes: keep a personal list of restriction enzymes beside your lab's stock, and choose how often an enzyme must cut to be shown separately from which list it comes from. Common, Lab and Mine are one row of buttons in the sidebar, and on the map they are in a menu beside the Cut sites button, which shows what is chosen. Any list can be used on the map, in the Sequence tab, and to choose enzymes for a digest or restriction cloning, and Find finds an enzyme from the chosen list even when the filter hides it.
  • A tidier toolbar: the sidebar's button is on the left and the inspector's on the right, beside the panels they show, and split view sits next to the views. Buttons that do nothing in the current view are greyed out. Under the map, the bar holds only rotate, zoom (with Fit) and label size.
  • Resizable sidebar and inspector: drag the inner edge of either panel to make it wider or narrower, in every view that has them, Reads included. The widths are kept for every file; double-click an edge for the usual width.
  • The map: each file's map opens turned the way you last turned it. Dragging round a circular map selects the bases the pointer passed over, whichever way it goes; dragging anticlockwise used to select nearly the whole plasmid. A coding sequence keeps its colour when another is added before it. On a Mac, labels sit in the middle of their coloured backgrounds, names written along a feature in the middle of it, and position numbers are centred too.
  • The Sequence tab stays where you are when you turn on Translation, Both strands, ORFs, Cut sites or Primers, or resize the window; it used to jump, further the further down you were. An enzyme that cuts right at a row's edge is shown beside the rest of its site, and codon shading alternates evenly again.
  • Sort more tables: click a heading to sort Home's constructs by name, length or date changed (within each folder), the Clones table by clone, how often it was seen, status or mutations, and the primer library by name, length, Tm or GC. A second click turns the order round; in the Clones table a third goes back to the order of your reads.
  • Hundreds of reads: the Reads tab's alignment draws only the reads near the view, so large sets of reads with traces use far less memory, and on a Mac no longer leave the traces blank. The ruler, features, translation and reference stay at the top while you scroll down through the reads, unless the pane is too short for both.
  • Read names in the alignment: reads from one plate leave out the start their names share, such as the date, so the well and primer show. With traces on, a long name wraps instead of being cut off. Drag the edge of the names column to resize it, or double-click it to fit the names.
  • Amino-acid changes from reads: two or three changes in one codon are now read together, so a codon swapped whole is reported as one change, such as A2W.
  • Library clones: clones with different CDRs are no longer counted as identical in the Clones table when the vector has Ns in its CDRs, or when two CDR3s have the same length.
  • Design primers a little away from the selection: the Design primers dialog has a "Move ends" distance. Allow the ends a few bases and each primer can slide outward onto a stretch that suits it, instead of being stuck on an AT-rich or GC-rich one at the edge of what you selected. The product only grows, so it still covers the whole region, and the pair nearest your selection wins when moving gains nothing. An end that gets a restriction site stays where it is. Candidates that bind more than one place or whose 3′ end pairs with itself are avoided, and the pair says so when nothing better fits.
  • Hide badly matched primer pairs: the Primers tab's Pairs list hides pairs whose two Tms are more than 5 °C apart, and says how many it hid. Set your own limit beside the list, or choose Show all to see them, marked.
  • Primers tab: clicking another primer in the sequence panel moves the panel to that primer, with its Tm, checks and handles, as clicking it in the list does. The PCR dialog gives the same Tm as the Primers tab.
  • Mutagenic primers: a primer whose change sits close to its 3′ end is no longer reported as not binding the sequence.
  • Saving right after typing: a primer's bases edited in the inspector are saved with every last keystroke, even when you save straight away, and saving while any edit is in flight no longer loses it. The last letters used to be left out of the file.
  • Gibson and In-Fusion check their overlaps more closely. The result says when two pieces already share their overlap, so the product keeps one copy of it, which matters when each was meant to carry its own linker or tag; to keep both, put both copies inside one piece and the join past them. It names a join whose overlap reads the same somewhere else in the product, on either strand, which is one way an assembly comes back rearranged, and one whose shared bases include anything other than A, C, G or T.
  • A piece too short to carry the whole overlap no longer loses bases: the plan joins on the bases the pieces really share, keeps every base of both, and says the overlap came out shorter than the method asks for.
  • Overlap melting temperatures in Gibson and In-Fusion are worked out by the Wallace rule, as the 48 °C guideline they are checked against was, and no longer change with the method chosen in Settings, which is for the temperatures shown elsewhere.
  • Markers on Home and in Info: NeoR/KanR counts as both kanamycin and neomycin, TcR as tetracycline resistance, and TetR, the repressor in Tet-On plasmids, no longer does.
  • On Windows, Ctrl+S saves again, and so do the other menu keys that did nothing there: Ctrl+Shift+S, Ctrl+N, Ctrl+O, Ctrl+W, Ctrl+, and the rest. The menu bar stays light, like the rest of the window, instead of turning dark when another window such as Settings opened.
  • Report a bug and Send feedback are now buttons at the top right of the window, beside the libraries, instead of only in the Help menu. Strandivo is still young, and a bug report, an idea or a question is the quickest way to shape what comes next. In a narrow window they show as icons.
  • Crash reports: if Strandivo quits unexpectedly, it notes why on your computer, and the next time it opens a strip under the app bar offers to report it. Report it… opens Report a Bug with what it noted, shown in full and with file paths removed; nothing is sent until you click Send. Recent errors are now kept for a week, so a report after a crash can still include them if you tick them.
  • No more "ResizeObserver loop" errors: this harmless notice, given when a panel resizes as it is drawn, no longer shows as an "Unexpected error" or goes into the recent errors a bug report can include.
  • Usage statistics, if you share them, count a little more, still only as fixed words and ranges: saving, adding features and primers, adding reads, how long a file's sequence is and how long alignment takes, and three choices in Settings. Each view is counted once each time the app opens, not at every switch. The Privacy page lists every event and what it carries.
  • Find accepts U in a sequence, and positions are written as 1,700–2,400 throughout.

Release 0.4.0 on GitHub

0.3.1

Fixes a crash in 0.3.0: with usage statistics turned on, Strandivo could quit on its own about half a minute after starting. If you turned statistics on, please update.

  • Sending usage statistics no longer makes Strandivo quit. It happened on every system whenever statistics were sent before that day's check for updates, which is most launches.

Release 0.3.1 on GitHub

0.3.0

Keep a list of your lab's enzymes and where they are, and show only those on the map. Map labels now tell features, primers and enzymes apart, and you can pick enzymes on the map to digest with. Send feedback or report a bug from the Help menu, and share anonymous usage statistics if you choose to.

  • Enzyme library: open Enzyme Library from the app bar or the Window menu to keep the enzymes your lab has, with each product's name, freezer location and notes. Choose Lab enzymes on the map or in the outline to show every site of the enzymes you stock and no others. Export and Import back the list up or share it, and Import also reads a spreadsheet saved as tab-separated or CSV text.
  • Map labels say what they name: a feature's name sits on a tint of its colour, a primer's follows an arrow pointing its way, and an enzyme's is small grey type, darker when it cuts once. Enzymes that cut once keep their labels when space runs short, and every site's position is in its tooltip.
  • An enzyme's other sites: selecting a cut site marks the enzyme's other sites on the map, in the Sequence tab and in the outline, and the inspector links to each one.
  • Pick enzymes to digest: ⌘-click (Ctrl-click on Windows and Linux) cut sites to pick their enzymes. Digest with… in the inspector, or on a right-click on any of their sites, opens the Digest dialog with them ticked. Esc or an ordinary click drops them.
  • Send Feedback… and Report a Bug… in the Help menu: everything sent is shown first, and recent errors (without file paths) and the open file go only if you tick them. Your email address, if you give one, and an attached file go to the maintainer alone, never into the public report. If a report can't be sent, you can copy it or email it instead.
  • The Help menu also has Strandivo Website and Strandivo on GitHub, and on Windows and Linux Welcome, Check for Updates and About.
  • Usage statistics stay off unless you turn them on. The welcome window asks once (if you have seen it before, it shows only this question), and Settings → Updates and privacy has the same switch, with what is sent. They are fixed words and rounded counts, never file names, sequences or anything you typed.
  • Update checks are now counted by day, version and system, to know how many copies are in use; nothing else about a check is kept.
  • strandivo.com has Download, What's new and Privacy pages; the Privacy page lists everything the app sends.

Release 0.2.1 on GitHub

0.2.0

Strandivo now updates itself: it checks once a day and installs a new version when you choose. A welcome window on first launch helps you add your construct folders and start a primer library.

  • Check for Updates… in the Strandivo menu (Help menu on Windows and Linux), and "Check for updates automatically" in Settings → Updates and privacy.
  • An update downloads in the background and installs when you restart; unsaved files are asked about first.
  • On Linux, both the AppImage and the .deb update themselves.
  • The welcome window can be opened again from Welcome to Strandivo… in the same menu.

Privacy

Strandivo is a desktop app, and your work stays on your computer. Your sequences, files, file names, folders, features, primers and notes are never sent anywhere by the app.

This page lists everything the app does send, and when. It is the one list: strandivo.com/privacy is made from it, so the two can't disagree. Strandivo is maintained by Mateusz Jaskolowski; questions about this page go to hello@strandivo.com.

Checking for updates

Once a day, while "Check for updates automatically" is on, and whenever you choose Check for Updates…, the app asks updates.strandivo.com whether there is a newer version. The question carries:

  • the app's version, such as 0.2.1;
  • the operating system, such as macOS or Windows;
  • the processor, such as Apple silicon or Intel.

Like any connection on the internet, it also comes from your network's address. The service answers with the newest version's details. All it keeps is a count: one more check that day for that version, operating system and processor, which tells roughly how many copies are in use. It doesn't record the address or anything that tells one copy from another, and it has no logs. It runs on Cloudflare, which carries the connection.

You can turn automatic checks off in the welcome window or in Settings → Updates and privacy. Check for Updates… still works when you ask.

Installing an update

When you choose to install an update, the app downloads it from GitHub, where Strandivo's releases are published. GitHub sees that download as it sees any other, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Looking up a protein in UniProt

When you look up a UniProt entry for a protein's regions, the app asks UniProt (rest.uniprot.org, run by EMBL-EBI and its partners) for that one entry, by its accession, such as P42212. Nothing else goes with it: not the sequence, not the file. It happens only when you ask. UniProt's own privacy notice covers what it keeps.

Sending feedback

Help → Send Feedback… and Report a Bug… send what you write to feedback.strandivo.com, only when you click Send. The dialog shows everything that goes with it before you send:

  • what kind it is (a bug, an idea or a question), your summary and your details;
  • the app's version, the operating system and its version, and the processor;
  • which part of the app was open, such as Home or the Map.

Two more things go only if you tick them, and both are off each time the dialog opens:

  • Recent errors: what went wrong in the app's windows in the past week, at most the last 20, with file paths removed. The dialog shows them as they will be sent. They are kept on your computer, so a report after a crash still has them, and dropped after a week.
  • The open file, as GenBank.

The service files your report as an issue in Strandivo's repository on GitHub, where anyone can read it, and tells you its number. Your email address, if you give one, and the open file, if you attach it, never go in the issue: they are emailed to the maintainer alone, with the issue's link, so the maintainer can answer you. The service keeps nothing itself and has no logs. To stop floods it lets each network send a few reports a minute, which Cloudflare counts for that minute and forgets. GitHub keeps the issue under GitHub's privacy statement; the email stays in the maintainer's mailbox. To have an issue or an email removed, write to hello@strandivo.com.

If a report can't be sent, nothing leaves the app: you can copy it, or email it to feedback@strandivo.com from your own mail app instead.

Reporting a crash

If Strandivo quits unexpectedly, it writes down why, in a small file in the app's own folder on your computer: the error's message, where in Strandivo's code it happened, which part of the app was running (its thread), the app's version, the operating system and the processor, and the time. Nothing is sent then.

The next time it opens, the app reads that file once and deletes it, and a strip under the app bar says "Strandivo quit unexpectedly last time." Dismiss forgets it. Report it… opens Report a Bug with Why it quit ticked and shown in full, as it will be sent: the message with file paths removed, where in the code, the thread, the version that quit and the time. It goes only if you click Send, as part of a bug report (see Sending feedback), and you can untick it first. Recent errors stay off unless you tick them.

Usage statistics

From Strandivo 0.3.0 the app can count which of its features are used, and on which systems, so that work goes where it helps most. It is off unless you turn it on: the welcome window asks, and "Share anonymous usage statistics" in Settings → Updates and privacy turns it on or off at any time. Until you turn it on, nothing is sent, and turning it off sends nothing either, not even that you turned it off; anything waiting to be sent is dropped at once.

When it is on, the app sends these events, and nothing else. Every detail an event carries is one of a few fixed words or a number put in a range, so none can hold a name, a sequence or anything you typed. A count's ranges are 0, 1, 2–5, 6–20 and more than 20; other numbers have their own few ranges, given below.

  • app_started: the app was opened, with arch, the processor (Apple silicon or ARM, Intel or AMD); installed, how long ago this copy was first opened (its first day, first week, first month or longer: the date itself stays on your computer); and folders, how many folders Home lists, as a range.
  • settings_chosen: once each time the app opens, three choices from Settings: tm, the Tm method; amber, whether TAG is read as Q; and bases, whether bases are shown in upper case, lower case or as in the file.
  • file_opened: a file was opened, with format, its kind (GenBank, SnapGene, FASTA, EMBL or other), worked out from its name, never the name itself; from, whether it was opened from Home, dropped on the window or opened another way; length, its sequence's length (under 5 kb, 5–15 kb, 15–50 kb or longer); and topology, circular or linear.
  • file_saved: a file was saved or exported, with format (GenBank or FASTA) and how (saved, or exported as a copy).
  • view_used: one of a file's five views was used, with view (Map, Sequence, Primers, Reads or Info); once a view each time the app opens.
  • feature_added: a feature was added, with how: from the selection, from an ORF or from a protein's region.
  • features_detected: known parts found in a sequence were added, with found, how many, as a range.
  • primer_added: a primer was added, with how: from the selection, typed in, or from the primer library.
  • primers_designed, pcr_product_made, digest_simulated: primer design, the PCR product dialog or a digest was used to the end.
  • mutagenesis_designed: mutagenesis primers were made, with layout, back to back or overlapping.
  • cloning_simulated: a cloning was simulated, with method (restriction, Gibson, In-Fusion or Golden Gate).
  • primer_pairs_shown, primer_pairs_exported: the Primers tab's Pairs view was shown, or its Export list was used.
  • reads_added: reads were added to a file, with format (AB1, FASTA, FASTQ, GenBank or a mix) and reads, how many, as a range.
  • reads_aligned: reads were checked against a sequence, with reads, how many, as a range; kind, whether Sanger reads, long reads or both; took, how long it took (under a second, 1–5 seconds, 5–30 seconds or longer); and variable, whether the sequence has variable regions.
  • check_saved: a read check was saved as a file.
  • update_installed: the app was updated to a new version.
  • error_caught: something went wrong in the app's window, with kind (a script error, a promise nobody caught, a command that failed, or a crash, counted when the app next opens); never the message, which could name a file.

Every event also carries the app's version, the operating system and its version, the time, and a session number: a random number the app makes when it starts and drops after four hours without events. It is never stored, so it can't link one day's use to the next. Events are sent every 30 seconds while the app is open. A test version (a release candidate, such as 0.4.0-rc.1) marks its events as tests, which Aptabase keeps apart from everyone else's. A copy built by the project's checks sends none, and neither does one built from the source, unless whoever builds it adds the project's key, which only its maintainers have; theirs are marked as tests too.

They go to Aptabase, an open-source service for app statistics, in its EU region. Like any server, it sees the address the events come from: it uses it to tell the country and region, and to count visitors a day with a code that changes every day, and it doesn't keep the address. Aptabase's own privacy policy covers what it keeps.

Opening the website

What's new… and some help items open strandivo.com or GitHub in your web browser. The app sends nothing itself; your browser visits the page as it would any other.

This website

strandivo.com has no cookies and no tracking. Its one script, on the home and Download pages, asks your browser which system it runs on, so that the right download comes first; it sends nothing and stores nothing. The site is served by Cloudflare, which handles each visit to deliver the page.

What the app doesn't do

  • No usage statistics unless you turn them on, and then only the events listed above.
  • No account, no sign-in, no identifier for your copy of the app.
  • No crash reports and no error messages are sent on their own: they go only in a bug report you send, and only as shown in it. With usage statistics on, an error or a crash is counted by its kind alone.
